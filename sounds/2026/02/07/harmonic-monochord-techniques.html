Harmonic Monochord Techniques

Feb 7, 2026

 

Tuning the monochord

The process of tuning itself is musically interesting. Tuning (or more exactly detuning) the pwm signals to match the actual (in)harmonic frequencies of the string produces various beatings and extra harmonics.



Natural harmonics

Using natural harmonics, i.e. lightly touching the string with a fingertip at certains nodes and/or antinodes, combines well with the “standard” way of playing the harmonic monochord. Certain harmonics can be muted, bringing the focus on other ones.

If the fingertip enters in contact with the vibrating string quickly enough, then an interesting buzzing effect appears.



Direct use of fingers on the string to mute the sound

The string vibration can also be abruptly stopped by pressing the finger completely onto the string until complete extinction of the sound. If the EM excitation is maintained, the release of the finger will free the build-up of the string resonance again. This technique can be use for a quicker control over the sound amplitude.



Change ringing patterns

The natural long release of the harmonics, especially in the higher register, gives to the sound a bell-like character. Experimenting with alternating patterns in a style reminiscent of change-ringing led to beautiful results.



Octaves

As one would expect, playing any pair of harmonics in a ratio of 2/1 will result in a sort of amplitude modulation effect, which can be altered and used for musical purposes either by playing with the volume or slightly detuning one or both harmonics of the pair.



High pitched harmonics

Higher harmonics being closer to each other (for instance the region between the harmonic #12 and #16) combine especially well and create interesting beating effects when played in polyphony.



Extra ringing

Certain combination of harmonics create additional resonances in the high-end of the spectrum. This effect is musically very interesting and adds another dimension to the sound.
